# Category-Specific Motion System

## 1. Purpose

Animation must communicate the identity of the business, not simply
make the page move.

This file is the canonical reference for deriving an industry-aware
motion concept for a client project. It is referenced by:

- `.agents/rules/03-animation.md`
- `design-system/motion.md`
- `docs/client-design-brief-template.md`
- `.agents/skills/new-client/SKILL.md`

Do not duplicate this content elsewhere. Link to this file instead.

---

## 2. The Core Distinction

> Animation should communicate the identity of the business.
> Not: animation should simply make the page move.

A generic motion layer (fade-up, stagger, hover-scale) applied
identically to a dental clinic, a motorcycle workshop, and a law firm
fails this standard even if every animation is individually smooth.

The category-specific motion system exists to prevent that failure
mode.

---

## 3. Motion Concept Derivation (Required Before Implementation)

For every client project, derive the motion concept using these six
questions, in order, before implementing animation. Record the answers
in the client design brief (Section 10, Motion Concept Derivation).

1. What physical or conceptual characteristics define this business?
2. What visual metaphors naturally emerge from those characteristics?
3. Which of those metaphors can become motion?
4. Which motion would reinforce the brand?
5. Which motion would improve storytelling or comprehension?
6. Which motion would simply be decoration, and should therefore be
   rejected?

Question 6 is not optional. Most candidate motion ideas should be
rejected at this step. The output of this process is a short,
specific motion concept, not a list of effects.

## 4. Industry Motion Banks

These are illustrative starting material, not templates. See Section 6
before using any of them.

### Dental / Dental Clinic

Physical material: teeth, enamel, smile, alignment, cleaning,
polishing, instruments, oral hygiene, treatment process,
before/after, diagnostic imagery.

Possible execution: refined tooth/enamel-inspired transitions, smooth
circular or polishing motion, subtle alignment concepts, before/after
reveals, precise clinical transitions, soft smile-related imagery
transitions, dental-diagram-inspired motion.

Avoid cartoon teeth or childish dental animation unless the brand
specifically targets children. The result should read as premium,
trustworthy, and clinical.

### Medical / Healthcare

Physical material: anatomy, diagnostics, healing, precision, care,
pulse/heartbeat where relevant, laboratory process, clean clinical
environments.

Motion should generally feel calm, precise, reassuring, and
controlled. Avoid dramatic or frightening medical animation.

### Barber / Salon / Grooming

Physical material: hair, scissors, clippers, flowing hair, styling,
transformation, before/after.

Possible execution: flowing image reveals, directional transitions,
precise cutting-inspired motion, before/after sliders, subtle
clipping metaphors, elegant typography movement. Should read as
stylish, not gimmicky.

### Automobile / Bike / Mechanical Workshop

Physical material: wheels, rotation, mechanical components, tools,
suspension, engines, torque, speed, precision.

Possible execution: controlled rotation, mechanical transitions,
directional movement, component-inspired reveals, technical line
animation, wheel/track-inspired transitions, service-process
storytelling. Motion can be stronger and more energetic here. Do not
add spinning objects merely because the business involves vehicles.

### Mobile / Electronics Repair

Physical material: devices, screens, circuitry, components,
diagnostics, connectivity, digital interfaces.

Possible execution: screen-like transitions, device-frame reveals,
circuit-inspired line movement, assembly/disassembly metaphors,
diagnostic-style transitions, precise micro-interactions. Keep the
visual language modern and technically sophisticated.

### Restaurant / Café / Food

Physical material: cooking, ingredients, pouring, steam, plating,
serving, texture, hospitality.

Possible execution: cinematic food reveals, ingredient transitions,
subtle liquid/pouring motion, image masking, plate/menu transitions,
atmospheric movement, elegant scroll storytelling. Avoid cartoon food
animation.

### Hotel / Hospitality / Resort

Physical material: arrival, travel, architecture, rooms, landscapes,
water, relaxation, luxury.

Possible execution: cinematic image transitions, slow atmospheric
movement, architectural reveals, immersive scrolling, subtle depth.
Motion should feel calm, luxurious, and spacious.

### Real Estate / Architecture / Construction

Physical material: buildings, floor plans, geometry, materials,
location, movement through rooms.

Possible execution: architectural line drawing, floor-plan
transitions, room-to-room storytelling, building reveals, geometric
transitions, map/location interaction, image masking. Motion should
communicate precision and spatial understanding.

### Fitness / Gym / Sports

Physical material: movement, strength, repetition, progression,
speed, energy, performance.

Possible execution: energetic transitions, progress indicators,
dynamic counters, movement-based reveals, strong directional
transitions, controlled scale/momentum. Intensity should match the
brand.

### Law / Legal Services

Physical material: authority, precision, structure, documents,
evidence, order.

Possible execution: precise typography reveals, document-inspired
transitions, structured movement, subtle line/geometry motion.
Motion should generally be restrained and authoritative — avoid
playful or excessive animation.

### Finance / Accounting / Professional Services

Physical material: data, growth, precision, stability, systems,
charts, transactions.

Possible execution: refined data visualization, subtle numerical
transitions, structured movement, chart reveals, progressive
information disclosure. Avoid cliché stock-market animation and
excessive futuristic effects.

### Technology / SaaS / Software

Physical material: systems, networks, interfaces, data, code,
connectivity, automation.

Possible execution: sophisticated interface transitions, data
movement, system diagrams, responsive micro-interactions, progressive
UI reveals. Do not automatically default to dark backgrounds, neon
gradients, glowing borders, or excessive glassmorphism.

### Education / Training

Physical material: learning, progression, discovery, knowledge,
achievement.

Possible execution: progressive content reveals, learning-path
animation, interactive diagrams, step-based transitions, educational
illustrations. Motion should support comprehension, not distract from
it.

### Travel / Tourism

Physical material: journeys, maps, routes, destinations, landscapes,
discovery.

Possible execution: route/path animation, map transitions, destination
reveals, cinematic imagery, journey-based scrolling.

### Photography / Creative Agency / Portfolio

Physical material: composition, imagery, framing, editorial layouts,
creative process.

This category can support more experimental motion when appropriate.
Possible execution: image masking, editorial transitions, cinematic
reveals, typography/image interaction, unconventional composition,
scroll storytelling.

## 5. Extending to a New Category

When the client's industry is not listed above, run the six questions
in Section 3 directly against the business rather than forcing it into
the nearest listed category. Add the resulting motion bank entry here
if it is likely to recur across future projects.

---

## 6. Category Motion Must Be Original

The banks above are inspiration and semantic material, not fixed
templates.

Two dental clinics should not automatically receive the same tooth
animation. Two restaurants should not automatically receive the same
food animation.

The client's brand, positioning, audience, location, personality,
price positioning, competitors, services, imagery, and content should
determine the final execution — not the category alone.

---

## 7. Avoid Literal or Gimmicky Interpretation

Category-derived motion should be sophisticated, metaphorical, subtle
where appropriate, visually coherent, brand-aligned, and memorable.

Avoid:

- cartoon animations
- literal objects flying around the page
- excessive 3D used only for spectacle
- decorative animation with no relationship to the content
- repetitive category clichés
- effects that make the website look like a template

Example — good: a dental clinic uses a refined circular transition
inspired by dental polishing.

Example — bad: a giant animated cartoon tooth continuously bounces
across the screen.

Example — good: a motorcycle workshop uses controlled rotational
transitions and mechanical component-inspired reveals.

Example — bad: every section contains a randomly spinning wheel.

---

## 8. Motion as a Competitive Differentiator

The goal is a visitor experience closer to:

> "This website feels like it belongs to this business."

than:

> "This website has some animations."

Treat category-derived motion as part of art direction, storytelling,
brand identity, interaction design, and conversion experience — not as
a decorative layer added at the end.

---

## 9. Final Category Motion Review

Before implementation, confirm:

- Has the motion concept been derived from the six questions in
  Section 3, not copied from the nearest industry bank entry?
- Would this motion concept transfer unchanged to a competitor in the
  same industry? If yes, it is not specific enough yet.
- Is every proposed motion idea tied to a genuine metaphor, or is it
  decoration that survived only because it looked technically
  impressive?
- Does the execution avoid literal, cartoonish, or clichéd
  interpretation (Section 7)?
- Is the motion documented in the client design brief before
  implementation began?

# Color System

## 1. Color Is Contextual

There is no universal Premium Website Builder color palette.

Every client requires a color strategy based on:

- brand
- industry
- audience
- emotional positioning
- geographic context
- cultural context
- trust
- accessibility
- conversion
- imagery

Do not automatically use the same colors for different clients.

---

## 2. Avoid Automatic AI Palettes

Do not default to:

- purple
- blue/purple gradients
- neon colors
- cyan
- excessive black
- excessive white
- pastel palettes
- glowing colors

These may be appropriate in specific contexts.

They are not universal defaults.

---

## 3. Color Psychology

Use color to support the intended emotional positioning.

Possible associations include:

- blue → trust, stability, professionalism
- green → health, nature, growth
- warm neutrals → comfort, craftsmanship, hospitality
- black → luxury, sophistication, authority
- earth tones → natural, grounded, artisanal
- red → energy, urgency, strength
- orange → warmth, energy, friendliness
- purple → creativity, luxury, imagination

These associations are contextual rather than absolute.

Do not choose colors solely from generic psychology charts.

---

## 4. Brand First

If the client has an established brand identity:

- preserve important brand colors
- improve their implementation where appropriate
- ensure accessibility
- create supporting colors around the brand

Do not replace an established identity unnecessarily.

---

## 5. Semantic Color Roles

Define colors by purpose rather than scattering arbitrary values.

Typical roles:

- background
- foreground
- surface
- surface-muted
- primary
- primary-foreground
- secondary
- secondary-foreground
- accent
- accent-foreground
- muted
- muted-foreground
- border
- input
- focus
- success
- warning
- error

---

## 6. Primary Color

The primary color should support the most important interactions.

It may be used for:

- primary CTA
- important links
- selected states
- key highlights

Do not use the primary color on everything.

---

## 7. Accent Color

Accent colors should draw attention selectively.

Use them for:

- important details
- highlights
- supporting interaction
- visual emphasis

An accent loses impact when used everywhere.

---

## 8. Backgrounds

Use background variation to establish hierarchy.

Possible approaches:

- neutral background
- tinted background
- alternating surfaces
- dark feature section
- image-led background

Do not add multiple backgrounds simply to create visual variety.

---

## 9. Gradients

Gradients are optional.

Use them when they support:

- brand identity
- depth
- atmosphere
- hierarchy
- imagery

Avoid generic gradient decoration.

---

## 10. Contrast

Maintain strong readability.

Check:

- body text
- headings
- buttons
- links
- muted text
- borders
- focus states

Do not use low-contrast text simply because it looks elegant.

---

## 11. Dark Themes

Dark themes are allowed when appropriate.

Do not make every premium website dark.

Dark themes should maintain:

- readable typography
- clear hierarchy
- appropriate contrast
- restrained effects
- accessible controls

---

## 12. Pastel Themes

Pastel colors are allowed when appropriate.

Do not use pastels merely because they are perceived as modern or
premium.

Pastels must still provide sufficient contrast and clear hierarchy.

---

## 13. Color Consistency

Once a client palette is established:

- use it consistently
- avoid arbitrary new colors
- use semantic tokens
- preserve contrast
- coordinate imagery with the palette

---

## 14. Final Color Review

Ask:

- Does the palette fit the client?
- Does it fit the audience?
- Is it distinctive?
- Is it accessible?
- Is the CTA hierarchy clear?
- Is there enough contrast?
- Is color being overused?
- Does the palette work with the imagery?
- Does it feel intentional rather than AI-generated?

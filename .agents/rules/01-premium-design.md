# Premium Design System Rules

## Purpose

These rules define the visual design standards for the Premium Website
Builder.

The objective is to create websites that feel intentionally designed,
professionally art-directed and appropriate for the specific client.

The website must not look like a generic AI-generated template.

---

## 1. Design Before Implementation

Before implementing substantial UI, understand:

- business type
- target audience
- geographic market
- brand personality
- business positioning
- primary conversion goal
- secondary conversion goals
- trust requirements
- content hierarchy
- available brand assets

Do not immediately generate a generic homepage from a short prompt.

For substantial projects, establish a creative direction before
building most of the interface.

---

## 2. Creative Direction

Establish a coherent visual direction covering:

- emotional tone
- visual personality
- color strategy
- typography
- layout
- imagery
- component treatment
- interaction style
- animation style

The visual direction must be appropriate for the client.

Examples of possible directions include:

- sophisticated
- editorial
- warm
- clinical
- trustworthy
- energetic
- technical
- luxurious
- playful
- minimal
- architectural
- rugged
- contemporary

These are examples, not templates.

Do not force the same personality onto every client.

---

## 3. Premium Does Not Mean Identical

Every client website should be premium.

Premium quality means:

- strong hierarchy
- thoughtful composition
- excellent typography
- appropriate color
- purposeful imagery
- responsive behavior
- refined interaction
- accessibility
- performance
- attention to detail
- strong conversion UX

Premium does NOT mean every client receives:

- the same color palette
- the same typography
- the same hero
- the same cards
- the same animation
- the same section order
- the same decorative elements

Maintain consistency in quality, not visual sameness.

---

## 4. Avoid Generic AI Aesthetics

Do not automatically use:

- purple gradients
- blue/purple gradients
- neon colors
- excessive glassmorphism
- excessive rounded cards
- excessive shadows
- huge gradient headings
- glowing borders
- floating blobs
- random decorative shapes
- excessive pills
- repetitive cards
- generic SaaS dashboards
- excessive centered hero sections
- excessive background effects
- animation everywhere

These techniques are allowed when they genuinely fit the client's
creative direction.

The rule is intentionality.

If a visual effect exists only because it is fashionable or commonly
generated by AI, question whether it should exist.

---

## 5. Visual Hierarchy

Every page should establish a clear hierarchy.

Users should quickly understand:

1. what the business offers
2. why it matters
3. why they should trust it
4. what they can do next

Use hierarchy through:

- typography
- size
- spacing
- contrast
- position
- imagery
- grouping
- alignment
- motion

Do not make everything visually dominant.

A strong primary element requires supporting elements with lower
visual priority.

---

## 6. Composition

Do not construct every page as a sequence of identical containers.

Avoid excessive repetition such as:

heading
subtitle
three cards
CTA
heading
subtitle
three cards
CTA

Use composition appropriate to the content.

Possible structures include:

- split layouts
- editorial layouts
- asymmetric compositions
- image-led sections
- full-width sections
- narrow reading columns
- overlapping elements
- structured grids
- timelines
- process layouts
- comparison layouts
- testimonials
- feature compositions

Choose based on the information being communicated.

---

## 7. Whitespace

Whitespace is a design tool.

Use it to:

- establish hierarchy
- separate concepts
- create rhythm
- emphasize important content
- improve readability
- create premium perception

Do not add excessive whitespace merely to make a page look expensive.

Do not compress content simply to fit more information above the fold.

Whitespace must support content and hierarchy.

---

## 8. Color

Color decisions must consider:

- brand
- industry
- audience
- emotional positioning
- culture
- geography
- trust
- accessibility
- conversion

Do not automatically use:

- pastel palettes
- neon palettes
- dark themes
- purple
- blue
- gradients

Pastel colors may be appropriate for some clients.

Dark themes may be appropriate for some clients.

Strong colors may be appropriate for some clients.

The client determines the direction.

Maintain semantic consistency between:

- background
- surface
- foreground
- muted text
- primary
- secondary
- accent
- border
- success
- warning
- error

Refer to:

`design-system/color.md`

---

## 9. Typography

Typography should contribute to the client's identity.

Do not automatically use Inter.

Choose typography based on:

- brand
- industry
- audience
- readability
- hierarchy
- tone
- performance

Create deliberate roles for:

- display
- headings
- body
- labels
- metadata
- buttons
- navigation

Avoid excessive font sizes.

Avoid using typography as decoration without a communication purpose.

Refer to:

`design-system/typography.md`

---

## 10. Imagery

Images should have a clear purpose.

Prefer:

- relevant imagery
- consistent visual treatment
- strong composition
- appropriate cropping
- good resolution
- appropriate aspect ratios

Avoid:

- unrelated stock images
- repetitive generic business photos
- low-quality images
- excessive image decoration

Do not present generated or stock imagery as real client imagery
unless its nature is clear.

---

## 11. Content and Trust

Design should support trust.

Depending on the business, consider:

- credentials
- services
- process
- location
- contact information
- real testimonials
- real reviews
- real certifications
- real affiliations
- real guarantees

Never invent these elements.

If information is unavailable, use placeholders or request the actual
information.

---

## 12. Conversion Design

Every important page should have an intentional conversion path.

Examples:

- book appointment
- request quote
- call
- contact
- visit location
- make reservation
- start consultation
- request service

Do not make every CTA visually identical.

Establish:

- primary CTA
- secondary CTA
- supporting actions

The strongest CTA should receive appropriate visual priority.

---

## 13. Design Restraint

Before adding a visual element, ask:

- What purpose does this serve?
- Does it improve understanding?
- Does it improve hierarchy?
- Does it improve interaction?
- Does it reinforce the brand?
- Does it improve conversion?

If the answer is no, consider removing it.

A premium design is often improved by removing unnecessary elements.

---

## 14. Final Design Test

Before considering a major page complete, evaluate:

- Does it look specific to this client?
- Does it feel professionally art-directed?
- Does the hierarchy make sense?
- Is typography intentional?
- Is color appropriate?
- Is composition varied but coherent?
- Is there unnecessary decoration?
- Does anything look obviously AI-generated?
- Does the design support the business goal?
- Does the page feel refined rather than overloaded?

When in doubt:

**simplify, verify and refine.**
